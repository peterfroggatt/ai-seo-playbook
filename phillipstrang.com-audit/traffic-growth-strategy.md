# phillipstrang.com — Strategy to Grow to High Traffic

## The core reframe: indexed count is not the goal, rankings are

Google does not reward you for having 1,645 indexed pages. It rewards pages
that *rank*. Right now:

- **1,513 pages produce 18% of clicks** (53 of 299) — and near-zero conversions.
- Those same pages depress the whole site's quality signal, which is **why your
  good pages sit at position 20–65 instead of page 1.**

So the question isn't "indexed pages vs traffic." It's "does the current sprawl
help or suppress ranking?" The data says suppress. Cutting the dead weight is
not *losing* traffic — it's removing the anchor that stops the rest from ranking.
Fewer, stronger pages that each rank on page 1 beat 1,645 pages that rank on
page 5. **Net traffic goes up as page count goes down** — this is the standard
content-consolidation playbook, and it works when (as here) the cut content is
thin, templated, and non-performing.

**But pruning alone just shrinks the site.** It's the enabler, not the engine.
The engine is the five moves below. The prune only pays off if we also do these.

---

## Move 1 — Consolidate, don't just delete (keep the equity)

Not all 1,513 vanish. Many *merge*:

- 20 near-duplicate "best [subgenre] novels" pages → a handful of comprehensive,
  authoritative guides that actually rank.
- Every merged/cut URL gets a **301 redirect** to its best surviving target, so
  any links or ranking equity transfer instead of evaporating.
- Result: you keep topical coverage but multiply per-page quality.

This is why the next deliverable is the **redirect-mapping sheet** — it makes the
cut a consolidation, not a deletion.

## Move 2 — Focus the topic to win crime-fiction authority

Google ranks *sites*, not just pages, on topical authority. A site that covers
crime **and** space opera **and** YA dystopian tells Google it's about nothing in
particular.

- **Cut all off-topic genres** (sci-fi, fantasy, horror, romance) regardless of
  their few clicks — they dilute your crime identity and never convert to a
  crime-novel sale.
- The payoff: a tightly crime-focused site becomes *the* crime-fiction resource
  in Google's eyes, which lifts **every** crime page's ranking at once.

## Move 3 — Strengthen the survivors (the ~130 keepers + new hubs)

- **On-page:** one relevant H1, tight titles, FAQ schema, real depth, your own
  books woven in with links (the top-10 brief starts this).
- **Hub-and-spoke internal linking:** pillar pages ("Crime novels by setting",
  "The crime-writing craft guide", "Scottish crime fiction") linking down to
  cluster pages and up from them. This concentrates authority onto the pages you
  want to rank instead of spreading it across 1,645 orphans.

## Move 4 — Build authority (this is the real traffic ceiling)

Your pages have decent content but rank at position 30–65. That's a **domain
authority** problem, not a content problem. This is the highest-leverage,
least-done work:

- **Backlinks:** author PR, guest posts on book/writing blogs, podcast
  interviews, book-blogger outreach, HARO/journalist queries. Every quality link
  lifts the whole domain.
- **E-E-A-T — your unfair advantage:** you are a *real, published crime
  novelist*. FantasticFiction and the AI-listicle farms can't claim that. Put a
  strong author bio, credentials, and first-person voice on every recommendation
  page ("I'm Phillip Strang, author of 40+ crime novels; here are the espionage
  thrillers I rate"). Genuine author expertise is exactly what Google's E-E-A-T
  and AI Overviews reward — and it's a moat competitors can't copy.

## Move 5 — Grow with intent-targeted NEW content (quality, not volume)

Replace "spray 1,600 templated pages" with "publish researched pages that can
actually rank":

- **Keyword research first:** target terms with real volume where you can
  realistically compete, clustered around your strengths — Scottish crime,
  Australian outback crime, police procedurals, crime-writing craft.
- **Every new page:** original author angle, genuine research, internal links,
  schema, your books woven in. 50 pages like this will out-traffic the 1,600
  that came before.
- **Cadence over blast:** steady, quality publishing signals a maintained,
  trustworthy site — the opposite signal to a one-time 1,600-page dump.

## Why this grows traffic even though pages drop

The math is in ranking position, not page count. CTR by position (roughly):

| Position | ~CTR | 900 impressions =>|
|---|---|---|
| 5 | ~7% | ~63 clicks |
| 20 | ~1% | ~9 clicks |
| 55–65 | ~0.1% | ~1 click |

Your Rebus page has **902 impressions at position 65 = 0 clicks.** Move it to
page 1 and that *one* page outperforms hundreds of the pages we're cutting. The
strategy trades 1,513 pages earning 53 clicks for ~130+ pages engineered to rank
on page 1 — plus new focused content on a de-risked, higher-authority domain.

## Safety & sequencing (de-risk the cut)

1. **noindex first, don't delete.** Bulk-`noindex` the CUT set — it's reversible
   and removes them from the index gradually. Watch 3–4 weeks.
2. **Confirm the core holds/improves,** then delete + 301 in waves (off-topic
   first, then zero-click).
3. **Keep monitoring GSC.** Double down on what climbs; this becomes ongoing
   hygiene, not a one-off.

## Realistic timeline & expectations

- Recovery from a helpful-content-suppressed state is **gradual — 3–9 months**,
  not instant. Set expectations accordingly.
- Short term (weeks): indexed count drops sharply; traffic dips slightly (~18%,
  much of it off-topic).
- Mid term (3–6 months): survivors climb as quality signals recover and internal
  links + authority compound.
- Long term (6–12 months): focused, authoritative crime site with new targeted
  content ranking on page 1 — designed to exceed current traffic several times
  over, and actually convert to book sales / KU reads.

## The honest caveat

Pruning + focus + authority + quality content is the highest-probability path,
and it's the established playbook — but SEO recovery is never guaranteed or fast.
The biggest risk is doing *only* the cut and not the authority/quality build.
The cut clears the ground; Moves 3–5 are what actually grow the traffic. Commit
to the whole machine, not just the prune.
