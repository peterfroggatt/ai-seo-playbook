# Top-10 Blog Improvement Brief — phillipstrang.com

Selected for **opportunity**: on-topic pages with real impressions stuck on
page 2–6, where climbing captures the most traffic. Data: Google Search
Console, ~4.5 months to 2026-07-04.

---

## The two structural levers that matter most (do these first)

Individual on-page tweaks give modest gains. Two things move low-authority
pages up more than any title edit:

1. **Internal links from your strongest pages.** Each of these 10 should be
   linked *to* from the homepage, the Phillip Strang Books hub, and 2–3 of your
   highest-authority posts, using descriptive anchor text (e.g. "best financial
   thriller novels"). Right now they mostly link *out* to your books but receive
   few links *in* — so they have little internal authority to rank with.
2. **The CUT of the 1,513 dead pages.** Removing that thin-content weight lifts
   site-wide quality signals and should help every page in this list rank. The
   prune isn't separate from this work — it's part of making these 10 win.

## Shared playbook — apply to all 10

- **Trim meta descriptions to ~155 characters.** Several are 165–235 chars and
  get truncated in Google. Lead with the benefit + the keyword.
- **Add FAQ schema.** None of the 10 have it. 3–4 real Q&As per page (the
  questions people actually ask) can win an AI Overview citation or a rich
  result — high leverage for informational queries like these.
- **Weave your books in.** Where your own titles aren't in the list/body, add
  them naturally with a link to the book page. This is the cross-pollination
  the whole blog was built for.
- **Confirm one relevant H1.** The snippet fixed counts, but two pages below
  have an H1 that doesn't match the topic — fix those by hand.
- **Refresh the date** on anything still saying 2025.

perfect intent: "books in order" readers are ready to be pointed somewhere.

  = exactly your **DI Sarah Lynch** series. Add a prominent "If you love Rebus,

  are there?", "What order to read Rebus?").

  climb.
